5.8 GHz Microstrip Patch Antenna
Design & Optimization

End-to-end design, simulation and optimization of a rectangular microstrip patch antenna at 5.8 GHz on an Arlon DiClad 870 substrate, matched to 50 Ω — from analytical sizing and 2D planar modelling in Ansoft Designer to full-wave 3D validation and tuning in CST Studio Suite.

← Back to projects

University Context

This project was developed for the course Antennas and Propagation (Prof. Sandra Costanzo, A.A. 2025/2026) at the Università della Calabria (UNICAL), Department of Computer Engineering, Modeling, Electronics and Systems (DIMES), together with Francesco Desando.

The work follows the real design flow of a planar antenna: closed-form sizing, 2D simulation under an infinite ground plane assumption, then a full-wave 3D model with a finite ground plane, which exposes non-idealities that the planar model cannot capture and must be compensated by optimization.

Key Results

ANSOFT DESIGNER

S11 ≤ −24 dB

Reflection coefficient minimum at 5.8 GHz after quarter-wave matching, under the infinite ground plane model.

CST STUDIO SUITE

S11 ≤ −20 dB

Match restored in 3D full-wave simulation with a finite ground plane after structural fine-tuning (L = 16.005 mm).

BANDWIDTH

1.50 % at −10 dB

From 5.758873 GHz to 5.845830 GHz around f₀ = 5.8 GHz, typical narrow-band behaviour of single-layer patches.

RADIATION

Broadside Main Lobe

Single lobe along +z (fundamental TM10 mode) with no significant back radiation thanks to the ground plane.

Objectives & Constraints

The goal is maximum power transfer from the source to the radiating element, i.e. minimising the reflection coefficient Γ = (Zp − Z₀)/(Zp + Z₀) at the operating frequency, and then verifying the far-field radiation characteristics.

Part I — Ansoft Designer

Planar (2D) modelling with an infinite ground plane. Initial dimensions come from the built-in Estimate tool (f₀, impedance, VSWR = 2), then the design is refined step by step.

STEP 1

Patch Only — Resonance

Simulated the bare patch (ground / Arlon substrate / copper patch) and iterated on L until Im{Zp(f₀)} = 0.

STEP 2

50 Ω Feed Line

Synthesised the feed width Wf and the guided wavelength λg, and set the line length to Lf = λg/2 so that Zin = Zp at the port.

STEP 3

Quarter-Wave Transformer

Read the patch edge impedance from the simulation and let the Estimate tool return Zt and the (Wt, Lt) section that transforms it into 50 Ω.

STEP 4

Simulation Settings

Frequency sweep from 4.5 to 7.0 GHz with 51 points and mesh frequency at 7.0 GHz for adequate spatial discretisation.

Results: at 5.80 GHz the input impedance is Re{Z} = 55.14 Ω and Im{Z} = 6.04 Ω, close to resonance and matching. |S11| reaches about −24 dB, and the radiation pattern (3D, rectangular and polar) shows a single broadside lobe along +z.

Part II — CST Studio Suite

The structure was rebuilt as a volumetric model with real thicknesses, conductivities and a finite ground plane. Substrate and ground are parametrically linked to the patch: total width 3L and total length 2L + Lt + Lf.

MODELLING

3D Parametric Workflow

Global variables imported from Designer, then finite ground plane, dielectric substrate, feed line, quarter-wave transformer, patch and excitation port.

DIAGNOSIS

Initial Mismatch

With the Designer dimensions, the finite ground plane introduces edge diffraction and fringe fields that completely detune the match at 5.8 GHz.

OPTIMIZATION

Parameter Sweep on L

Several scan intervals were explored iteratively to map the sensitivity, converging on L = 16.005 mm with Re{Zin} ≈ 50 Ω, Im{Zin} ≈ 0 Ω and Γ ≤ −20 dB.

VALIDATION

Pre- vs Post-Sweep

Superimposed curves show the S11 notch recentred at 5.8 GHz and the reactive component cancelled, with a −10 dB bandwidth of 1.50 %.

Conclusions

The project shows that 2D analytical and planar synthesis is an excellent starting point (S11 ≤ −24 dB under an infinite ground plane), but that a full-wave 3D optimization is mandatory before fabrication: the finite ground plane shifts the resonance and breaks the match.

Successive parameter sweeps on the patch length compensated for the edge effects and yielded the executive dimensions for a prototype, with S11 ≤ −20 dB and a 1.50 % fractional bandwidth at 5.8 GHz — consistent with the intrinsic narrow-band nature of single-layer patch antennas.
